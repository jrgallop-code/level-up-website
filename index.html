THE SCIENCE BEHIND THE COACHING

Evidence first.
Adjustments second.

Level Up is designed around principles supported by resistance-training research, then applies them conservatively to your own data. It does not assume there is one perfect rep range, one perfect split, or a reason to change something every week.

01 · PROGRESSION

Progress the stimulus when performance supports it.

Progressive resistance training is a core recommendation in the current ACSM position stand. Level Up ties load guidance to what you actually completed instead of automatically adding weight because another week passed.

02 · VOLUME

Weekly muscle volume is worth paying attention to.

Research supports a relationship between resistance-training volume and hypertrophy, with current evidence favoring multiple weekly sets rather than treating every program as equivalent. Level Up makes muscle-set distribution visible so volume is something you can actually manage.

03 · FLEXIBILITY

Hypertrophy is not locked to one magic load or rep range.

Meta-analyses show muscle growth can occur across a broad range of training loads. Level Up uses your programmed rep range as a practical progression target — not as a claim that one narrow rep range is the only way to build muscle.

PERFORMANCE-BASED PROGRESSION

The app coaches the set you are actually doing.

Level Up compares your performance with the rep range you programmed and your previous workout. It does not blindly tell you to add weight every week — it waits for the performance to justify the recommendation.

  • Reach the top of your programmed range across completed working sets and Level Up can recommend a load increase
  • If most completed sets fall below the range, it can suggest reducing the load instead of forcing progression
  • One low set does not trigger a reduction — Level Up looks for a pattern before reacting
  • Your previous non-blank performance stays beside today’s sets so the recommendation has context

FAQ

What does “science-based” actually mean?

What makes Level Up science-based?

Level Up’s training rules are designed around evidence-informed resistance-training principles such as progressive resistance, managing weekly training volume, using a range of effective loading approaches, and making changes from performance data rather than arbitrary schedules. The app itself has not been tested as a complete clinical intervention, so we use “science-based” to describe the principles behind the system rather than claiming the entire product has been clinically validated.

Is Level Up a human or AI coach?

No. Level Up is a training app with coaching-style decision rules built around the data you log. It does not pretend to replace a qualified human coach, and it does not make random changes. It surfaces recommendations when your performance or weight-trend data meets the conditions for an adjustment.

How does workout progression guidance work?

Level Up compares completed working sets with the rep range programmed for that exercise. When every completed working set reaches the top of the range, it can recommend a practical load increase. If most completed sets fall below the range, it can instead suggest a lighter load.

Why track weekly muscle volume?

Training volume is one of the resistance-training variables linked with hypertrophy outcomes in the literature. Level Up tracks weekly muscle-set distribution so you can see whether the program is actually allocating work where you intended.

How does the nutrition coach adjust calories?

During an active nutrition phase, Level Up waits for enough weigh-ins to establish a trend. Scheduled check-ins compare your actual 7-day moving-average change with your target weekly pace and can recommend a calorie adjustment when the difference is large enough.
